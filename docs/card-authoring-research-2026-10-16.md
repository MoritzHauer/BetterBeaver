# Card authoring: what to cull, and who culls it

A short survey, 2026-10-16, before L2–L9 of the software-architecture Book ([plan 0028](plans/0028-software-architecture-books.md)) are written. The question: what do established card-writing guides and published LLM card-generation workflows put between "drafted" and "shipped", and what of that is worth adding to `/ingest`? The answer became [`/card-review`](../.claude/skills/card-review/SKILL.md), an independent reviewer that keeps, rewrites, splits, moves or cuts every freshly drafted card before validation.

## What already existed

- **Rules for writing:** [content-practices.md](content-practices.md) §§1–6 cover unit size, one-sentence definitions, which exercises a domain allows, task shapes, and the question rules (every distractor a real misconception, a `why` on misleading options).
- **Mechanical gates:** the validator, `overlap-check.py` (own words) and `card-check.py`. The last one is a **learner's** lens: a second model answers each card from its front and flags AMBIGUOUS or wrong answers, and plain checks catch a front that contains its answer.
- **What was missing:** an **editor's** lens. card-check asks whether a card _can be answered_, never whether it is _worth answering_: whether it holds one fact or four, serves the unit's objectives, collides with a card two units back, or tests a label instead of understanding. Run over the shipped L0/L1 cards, card-check flagged 5 of 69 cards. The review kept 10 of them (see [Demonstration](#demonstration-on-l0l1)).

## Sources

1. P. Wozniak, [_Effective learning: Twenty rules of formulating knowledge_](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/twenty-rules-of-formulating-knowledge) (SuperMemo, 1999). Among them: the minimum-information principle (#4), avoid sets (#9) and enumerations (#10), combat interference (#11), optimize wording (#12), context cues (#16), prioritize (#20).
2. A. Matuschak, [_How to write good prompts_](https://andymatuschak.org/prompts/). A good prompt is focused, precise, consistent, tractable and effortful. Make one prompt per list item. Write more small prompts than feels natural, and skip the ones you don't care about.
3. A. Matuschak, working note [_In prompt generation, LLMs may perform better when given prompt-writing principles_](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zPXE9jtKyU7AQRBmAUQrbWQ). It is informal, and its author calls the effect unreliable.
4. M. Nielsen, [_Augmenting Long-term Memory_](https://augmentingcognition.com/ltm.html) (2018). Make cards as atomic as possible. Avoid orphan questions. Treat compound and yes/no questions as smells. Don't memorize dubious claims.
5. [_Creating Flashcards with LLMs_](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/hGhBhLsgNWLCJ3g9b/creating-flashcards-with-llms) (LessWrong). Ask for more cards than you want, then delete. Typical failures: trivial cards, an answer that restates the front, "according to the author", and references to context the learner can't see.
6. [_Comparing GPT-4, 3.5 and some offline local LLMs at the task of generating flashcards_](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2024/01/comparing-gpt-4-3-5-and-some-offline-local-llms-at-the-task-of-generating-flashcards-for-spaced-repetition-e-g-anki/) (R-bloggers, 2024). Its rubric: self-contained, atomic, makes sense as a flashcard, true, "would I use it". **21 of 100 generated cards** made it into the author's deck.
7. [_AI-generated Anki cards are not to be trusted_](https://denovo.substack.com/p/ai-generated-anki-cards-are-not-to) (de novo). A generated card reversed a gradient's direction. A second model missed the error even when pointed at the card, and it flagged a correct card instead.
8. S. Moore, H. A. Nguyen, T. Chen, J. Stamper, [_Assessing the Quality of Multiple-Choice Questions Using GPT-4 and Rule-Based Methods_](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08161) (ECTEL 2023). It uses a 19-flaw item-writing rubric (implausible distractors, longest option correct, absolute terms, word repeats, negative stems and others). Rules caught **91 %** of the flaws human raters found; GPT-4 caught **79 %**.
9. A. Panickssery, S. Bowman, S. Feng, [_LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations_](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076) (NeurIPS 2024). Models rate their own text above others' of equal quality.
10. Published card-generation skills and tools. [anki-card-skill](https://github.com/gong1414/anki-card-skill) and [a Claude + Anki workflow](https://www.johnwhiles.com/posts/claude-anki) put the principles into the **generation** prompt. [RecallFlow](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/recallflow-ai-an-ai-powered-obsidian-plugin-for-flashcards-and-fsrs-based-spaced-repetition/117087) has a human preview. None of the ones found has a separate culling agent. The "checking agent that throws most cards away" is the pattern sources 5 and 6 describe as a _human_ habit, moved into its own model run.
11. Claude Code, [Skills: `context: fork`](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills). A forked skill runs in a subagent that "doesn't see your conversation history". That is the mechanism the reviewer's independence rests on.

## Ideas, and what became of them

**Adopted** = new in this change · **Covered** = already in the process · **Rejected** = deliberately left out.

### The workflow

| Idea                                                       | Source | Verdict               | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A separate pass that culls drafted cards                   | 5, 6   | **Adopted**           | `/card-review`, the step after drafting and wiring, before the gates. Cutting is the expected outcome, not a failure: a marginal card costs every learner review time for as long as they keep the Book. |
| The reviewer must not share the author's context           | 9, 11  | **Adopted**           | `context: fork` gives a subagent with no drafting history. The read-only `card-reviewer` agent proposes changes, and only the author edits.                                                              |
| Review with a different model family                       | 9      | **Rejected**          | The harness runs Claude only. The fresh context, the explicit criteria and card-check's second model (a different tier) are the mitigations.                                                             |
| Mechanical flaws by rule, judgment by model                | 8      | **Adopted**           | card-check gains one rule: every correct option clearly longer than every wrong one. Absolute words and grammatical cues need judgment, so they go to the reviewer (Q3).                                 |
| A model's factual verdicts are unreliable both ways        | 7      | **Adopted**           | The reviewer never rewrites a fact. A doubtful claim gets an OWNER verdict for the owner to settle, and cards are judged against the unit's note, not the reviewer's memory.                             |
| Generate more than you need, then delete                   | 5      | **Adopted, softened** | Own-words drafting is expensive, so there is no overproduction quota. But don't pad to reach a count, and don't hold back a doubtful candidate: let the review decide.                                   |
| Give the generating model the principles                   | 3      | **Covered**           | `/ingest` already makes the author read content-practices, which now carries the culling criteria in brief (§8).                                                                                         |
| Make the author explain how each card meets each principle | 3      | **Rejected**          | Self-justification is the biased check that source 9 warns about. The independent review replaces it.                                                                                                    |
| Track the cut rate per batch                               | 5, 6   | **Adopted**           | Each publish reports a review tally per unit. A criterion that keeps firing means the drafting is wrong, so it becomes a line in content-practices. card-check's flag rate already works this way.       |
| Learner feedback after shipping                            | —      | **Covered**           | `pull-feedback.ts` lists 👎 and reported cards.                                                                                                                                                          |
| N/100 cards per N words of source                          | 5      | **Rejected**          | Unit sizing (content-practices §1) governs, not source length.                                                                                                                                           |

### What a card must survive

| Idea                                                | Source     | Verdict                    | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Minimum information: one thing to remember per card | 1, 2, 4, 6 | **Adopted** (A1)           | In this Book a definition is also the back of a self-graded `recall` card, so a back with three facts can't be graded honestly. Shipped example: `iSAQB` packs association, founding year, curricula and independent examiners into one definition.                                                              |
| Avoid sets and enumerations                         | 1, 2       | **Adopted** (A2)           | A list-shaped definition (`Nutzen von Softwarearchitektur`: four benefits) is rewritten to the idea that unites the items. The list moves into a `choice` question, which judges each item on its own, the way the exam asks for sets. A closed, named set that is itself the fact (magisches Viereck) may stay. |
| Prioritize: only what serves the goal               | 1, 2       | **Adopted** (A3)           | Each card serves an LZ id in its unit's goal, or it is moved into the note or cut. Trivia such as founding years goes.                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Learn before you memorize; avoid orphans            | 1, 4       | **Adopted** (A4)           | Each card retrieves something its unit's note teaches. content-practices §5 already required this of questions. Now concepts are held to it too.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Precise, consistent prompts                         | 2          | **Adopted** (A5, C1)       | The precise-prompt criterion also catches **author labels used as terms**: `Architektur und Betrieb` or `Anforderungen klären` tell a learner on `recall` nothing about what to retrieve. content-practices §3 already banned `write` on such terms. Now the concept itself is questioned.                       |
| Combat interference                                 | 1          | **Adopted** (A6)           | card-check sees one unit at a time. The reviewer reads the whole domain lexicon. That matters from L2 on: `Kapselung` (L1) against `Information Hiding` (L3), and `Kopplung` against `Kohäsion`.                                                                                                                 |
| Don't give the answer away                          | 2, 5       | **Covered + adopted** (A7) | card-check catches the whole term inside its definition. The reviewer also catches the part of the term that gives it away (`Softwareintensives System`: "System, dessen Zweck ohne seine Softwareanteile …").                                                                                                   |
| Effortful, not trivial                              | 2          | **Adopted** (A8)           | Cut a card answerable from everyday sense, or one whose single plausible option gives it away.                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Self-contained, no "according to the author"        | 5, 6       | **Adopted** (A10)          | No references to the textbook's chapters, figures or running example. That is also plan 0028 §3's rule from the learner's side.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Optimize wording                                    | 1          | **Adopted** (A10)          | Shorter when nothing is lost, and the Book's own terms used consistently.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Short, standalone definitions                       | —          | **Covered**                | content-practices §2, plus card-check's 25-word check.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Context cues to disambiguate                        | 1          | **Covered** (L1)           | For lexemes the gloss carries the cue. For concepts the term already includes an English cue where needed ("Sicht (View)").                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Several angles on one idea                          | 1, 4       | **Covered**                | The concept trio (board → MCQ → flashcard) plus the unit's questions is that redundancy.                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| One gap per cloze review                            | 2          | **Adopted** (L3)           | Kyrgyz cloze sentences gap the form the unit teaches, one thing per gap.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Provide sources, date stamps                        | 1          | **Covered**                | `sourceRef` and the resource entries carry source and version.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Imagery, graphic deletion, mnemonics, emotion       | 1          | **Rejected**               | The public Book reproduces no figures (plan 0028 §3), its tasks are text-only, and mnemonics are personal.                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

### Questions: the item-writing flaws (source 8)

| Flaw                                                           | Verdict                            | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Implausible distractor                                         | **Covered + adopted** (Q2)         | content-practices §5 already required a real misconception. The reviewer now checks it.                                                                                                                               |
| Longest option correct                                         | **Adopted**                        | A card-check script rule, and Q3 for subtler length or detail cues. Three shipped questions trip it: in sa-item-q-1-4-1 the three correct options are the three longest.                                              |
| Absolute terms, grammatical cues, word repeats, convergence    | **Adopted** (Q3)                   | Test-wise cues: "immer" appears only in distractors in two shipped questions.                                                                                                                                         |
| Ambiguous; more than one correct (single-answer)               | **Covered + adopted** (Q4)         | card-check asks a model, and the reviewer asks whether an expert could argue another option.                                                                                                                          |
| Gratuitous information, negative stem, "none/all of the above" | **Adopted** (Q6)                   |                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Unfocused stem                                                 | **Adopted for single-answer only** | A P-question's "Welche zwei Aussagen … treffen zu?" is the CPSA-F exam's own format, which the Book prepares for.                                                                                                     |
| True/false series; complex/K-type                              | **Rejected**                       | The exam's K-questions are exactly that, and `assign` mirrors them. Nielsen's yes/no smell (source 4) is rejected for the same reason. What survives of it is Q5: one claim per row.                                  |
| Lost sequence (options out of order)                           | **Rejected**                       | The app shuffles option order since `afce0e0`.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Fill-in-blank stems                                            | **Rejected**                       | No question here is written that way.                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| A question that just restates a concept card                   | **Adopted** (Q1, ours)             | It has no flaw-rubric equivalent. The rubric judges a question alone, but here it sits next to the concept trio that already drills the definition. "Was besagt das Twin-Peaks-Modell?" asks the `recall` card again. |

## Demonstration on L0/L1

`/card-review` was run once per unit over the five live units: 69 cards, 54 concepts and 15 questions, as published at `topic:softwarearchitektur` version 2. Nothing was changed. The per-card verdicts are in the PR description.

| Unit                                       | Cards  | Keep   | Rewrite | Move   | Cut   | Owner |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------ | ------ | ------- | ------ | ----- | ----- |
| 0-1 Zertifikat, Relevanzstufen, Fragearten | 14     | 1      | 10      | 1      | 2     | 0     |
| 1-1 Softwareintensive Systeme              | 13     | 3      | 8       | 1      | 1     | 0     |
| 1-2 Bausteine und Schnittstellen           | 13     | 2      | 8       | 2      | 1     | 0     |
| 1-3 Beschreibung, Umgebung, Güte           | 13     | 3      | 9       | 0      | 0     | 1     |
| 1-4 Entwerfen und zusammenarbeiten         | 16     | 1      | 5       | 7      | 3     | 0     |
| **Total**                                  | **69** | **10** | **40**  | **11** | **7** | **1** |

That is 86 % not kept, against card-check's 7 % flagged on the same cards. The rate is high, and the cards were written before these criteria existed. The finding classes that recur are the ones to fix in drafting:

- **Questions that re-ask a concept card (Q1)** — 10 of 15. "Was besagt das Twin-Peaks-Modell?" and assign rows copied from the definitions. The proposed rewrites turn them into situations.
- **Author labels as terms (C1)** — the eleven task and role cards of L1.4. Seven MOVE into two new questions, three are cut, and one is renamed to a real term. The reviewer then notes that the unit falls below `recognize`'s floor of four concepts.
- **List-shaped definitions (A2)** — `Nutzen von Softwarearchitektur`, `Software`, `Stakeholder`, `Schnittstelle`.
- **Notes that don't teach what a card drills (A4)** — for example, `Implizite Architektur` is never named in its note.
- **Cross-unit interference (A6)** — `Technische Architekturebene` (L1.3) against `Querschnittsthemen entscheiden` (L1.4), and `Kapselung` against `Blackbox-Sicht`.
- **Single real defects** — q-0-1-1 says "75 Minuten", which the note contradicts for non-native speakers (Q4). A `why` in q-1-1-2 contradicts unit 1-2's definition of Softwarearchitektur. `Architekturstil` was sent to the owner as a doubtful definition (A9). A choice task's "Wähle alle Antworten" hangs over a single-answer question.

**Calibration.** The first run over unit 1-1 kept 1 of 13. It flagged every word the note doesn't repeat, and it judged LZ coverage it cannot see, because the LZ texts are deliberately absent. A3, A4 and A7 were narrowed, and a finding must now name what a learner would get wrong. The second run kept 3 of 13. Whether that is still too strict is the first thing to judge on the L2 run: if authors keep declining one criterion, narrow it here.
